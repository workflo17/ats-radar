The Stage Two Playbook

Everything you get paid for runs through one definition: a meeting you booked, that the prospect attended, that the AE moved to Develop. This is the plan for each of those terms, the four tools that act on them, and what has to happen before the 28th.

01 / The mechanics

Your pay is a five-term product

Miss on any single term and the whole month collapses, which is why volume alone does not carry the role. Most SDR tooling attacks the first two stages. The tools below are weighted toward the last two, because those are the ones your commission agreement actually names.

STAGE 1 Accounts worked Who is on a competitor ATS, and who changed this week. TOOL 1 · radar
STAGE 2 Reply The message itself. No tool saves you here. your craft
STAGE 3 Meeting booked Calendar mechanics, and a confirmation that carries content. TOOL 2 · brief
STAGE 4 Prospect attends Benchmark show rate is 75 to 80%. Below that, the confirmation flow is broken. TOOL 2 · brief
STAGE 5 AE moves to Develop Decided on whatever context the AE has ten minutes before the call. TOOL 3 · handoff
TOOL 4 · ledger Records every stage transition underneath all five, because attainment is computed from a Salesforce field you do not control.

03 / Week one

Six things to get from Amanda

The commission agreement leaves out most of the numbers that determine your pay. Ask in the first one-to-one, and ask for the answers in writing.

  • Your monthly quota. The plan says "as defined by the Employee's manager and Segment" and points at Salesforce. Get the number, and confirm it is set in advance rather than retroactively.
  • The Ramping Period. Referenced in Sections II.D, III and V, defined nowhere. How long, what the ramped targets are, and what the "applicable target percentage" is during ramp.
  • "Created opportunity" versus "Stage Two Opportunity." Quota runs on Stage Two. The 70 to 99% shortfall payment pays per "created opportunity," which is undefined and looser. This changes your math in exactly the months you care most.
  • What happens when an AE sits on it. A meeting only counts once someone else changes a picklist. Ask what the recourse is.
  • Sales Contract Bonus timing. It needs 70% attainment "in that same month," but a deal you source in October may close in February. Same month as the close, or as the sourcing?
  • The 2027 plan. The current agreement terminates 31 December 2026 and does not auto-renew. Your $2,000 a month rides on whatever replaces it.
04 / The kit

Six tools, already built

All of it reads public job boards and writes text. Nothing touches Greenhouse's Salesforce, and nothing sends email on its own.

1

Displacement radar

Every major ATS publishes its customers' job boards as a free unauthenticated JSON feed. Poll them daily and you know who runs a competitor, how fast they are hiring, and what changed overnight. Vendors sell the state and lag 90 to 180 days on mid-market records. You own the change, same day. 

# runs itself at 06:30; these are for when you want it now
npm run collect     # poll every target
npm run report      # ranked call list
npm run diff        # what changed since yesterday
2

Territory ingest

Amanda will hand you company names, not domains, and the collector needs to find board tokens. This reads whatever shape the CRM exports, strips the legal suffixes a board token never carries, resolves each company to its ATS, and routes the failures to a fix-by-hand file. A website column is optional: board tokens come from the company name at least as often as from the domain. 

npm run ingest -- --file territory.csv --dry-run   # look first
npm run ingest -- --file territory.csv --replace
3

Show-rate pack

Three touches plus a pre-call one-pager built from the prospect's own board. An instant personal confirmation cuts no-shows by around 40%; a personalised pre-call asset moves close rates on positive replies from 8.4% to 31.2%. On twelve booked meetings against a quota of ten, the gap between a 70% and an 88% show rate is the gap between missing and clearing. 

npm run brief -- --account ramp.com --contact "Jane Doe" \
  --when "Tue Oct 14, 2:00pm ET" --ae "Sam Rivera"
4

AE handoff brief

One page: who, why now, what they actually said, their board from the outside, and four questions drawn from what that board shows. The battlecard for whichever ATS they run is attached automatically, including where that competitor genuinely beats Greenhouse, because a prospect evaluating Ashby has already read Ashby's website. The second-order effect is the bigger one: AEs talk about which SDR makes their life easier, and that is how territory gets allocated. 

npm run handoff -- --account ramp.com --contact "Jane Doe" \
  --trigger "Closed their Head of Talent Ops req 3 weeks ago" \
  --notes "On Ashby. Reporting across sources is the pain."
5

Attribution ledger

Append-only log with the commission agreement's arithmetic built in: the 70% floor, the 150 / 175 / 200% accelerator bands, and the Sales Contract Bonus caps. Reconcile it against Salesforce before every payroll date. 

npm run ledger -- add --account ramp.com --contact "Jane Doe" --when 2026-10-14
npm run ledger -- advanced k3f9x2 --ae "Sam Rivera"
npm run ledger -- month 2026-10   # attainment, show rate, what you are owed
6

Loop closing

The one nobody else on the team can run, because nobody else has been collecting since September. Joins your ledger against the snapshot history to find which signal actually produced advanced opportunities, whether 35 days is the right call-on delay, and where the funnel really leaks. It refuses to print a percentage below five samples, so it will tell you nothing useful until about December. That is the honest answer, not a limitation. 

npm run learn
05 / What to act on

The signal ladder

The radar emits eight change events, priority-ranked. The top one is the reason the daily collection matters: a senior talent req closing means they hired, senior hires start four to eight weeks out, and the report hands you a dated reminder that lands in week two of the new person's tenure while they are still writing the list of things that are broken.

10TALENT_LEADER_HIREDA senior talent req closed. Emits a call-on date 35 days out.
9ATS_MIGRATIONThey moved platforms. Just churned off a competitor, or mid-evaluation.
8TALENT_LEADER_POSTEDThe buyer is being recruited right now.
7REPOSTClosed a req and posted it again. They cannot fill it.
6VELOCITY_SPIKEHiring accelerating past what their process handles.
5TA_TEAM_GROWINGTwo or more new recruiter reqs.
4BOARD_APPEAREDStarted hiring properly where there was nothing.
3BOARD_EMPTIEDFreeze or cleanup. Worth knowing before you call.

One meeting is the difference between $0 and $1,400 at the bottom of that table. That single row is the entire argument for tool 2. Median Greenhouse contract value runs about $26,600, so the Sales Contract Bonus at 1% adds roughly $270 per closed deal; the $5,000 per-deal cap would need a half-million-dollar contract and is not reachable at your tier. The money is in meeting volume and the accelerators.

07 / Operating rhythm

First ninety days

Every morning, ten minutes

  • Read the overnight diff. Work the priority 10s and 9s first, they are dated and they expire.
  • Check the ledger for meetings still marked booked from yesterday. Resolve them.
  • Then close the laptop and dial.

Week one

  • Get the six answers from Amanda. Put the real quota into config/comp.json.
  • Run npm run ingest on the territory export. Check data/unresolved.csv and fix those by hand.
  • Ask whether custom tooling is welcome. Most sales orgs love it; a few have policies. Frame it as built before you started, reads public data only.
  • Learn the product well enough to talk to a technical talent-ops buyer. You can read Greenhouse's own API docs, which most SDRs never do.

First thirty days

  • Send the handoff brief for every single meeting, without exception. This is how you become the SDR the AEs ask for.
  • Run the full three-touch confirmation on every booking. Track your show rate weekly.
  • Log every opportunity the same day. The ledger is worthless if it is retroactive.

Days thirty to ninety

  • You now have a quarter of change data nobody else on the team has. Start working --days 30 diffs for reposts.
  • Tune the scoring in report.mjs against what actually converted.
  • Reconcile the ledger against Salesforce before every payroll date.
  • Push for the Senior SDR tier. It moves the Monthly Role Bonus to $2,200 and doubles the contract bonus rate.
08 / Guardrails

Two rules that do not bend

Data flows one direction

Public web in, ranked list out. Nothing from Greenhouse's Salesforce, customer records or internal data ever enters this repo. CIIA 1.1 says company information "cannot be downloaded or retained for my personal use," and CIIA 9 lets them inspect a personal machine used for company data. Keep the tools reading public APIs and that exposure never exists. Never use a personal device for Greenhouse work.

The real risk is building instead of selling

You have fourteen active projects. The failure mode for a builder in a sales job is that tooling feels productive right up until the month closes short. These four tools are maybe a fifth of what makes an SDR successful. The rest is call volume, talk tracks, the same six objections until they are reflex, and being the person AEs want to work with. The tools are finished. Go make calls.
